TrailBuddy — Privacy policy

As of 31 October 2026. Applies to the Android app and the web app at macbuchi.github.io/trailbuddy/.

This is a courtesy translation. The legally binding text is the German version; where the two differ, the German one applies.

Controller

Marcus Bucher, Schumannstr. 7, 74906 Bad Rappenau, Germany. Contact: pilzbuddy@proton.me. Full details in the legal notice.

The short overview

  • TrailBuddy stores your account (email, username), your buddy connections and the trail recordings you contribute: the line of a trail with its time and source.
  • Whole rides never leave your device on their own. Only the sections you pick as a trail are contributed. If you export a ride or a trail as a GPX file, you pass it through your system's share menu to wherever you want — TrailBuddy transfers nothing in the process.
  • Your trails are visible only to you and the buddies you are connected with. There is no public map, no trail search and no analysis across all users.
  • The data is stored with Supabase (processor, region Frankfurt am Main). When the map loads, your IP address goes to Cloudflare (where the map and the places on the map are stored); when checking for updates, to GitHub.
  • No advertising, no tracking, no sharing with third parties for their own purposes.

What a recording is — and what it is not

A recording is a piece of path: start, end, direction, the line in between with its elevations, and the time. From the elevations the app calculates climbing, gradient and the elevation profile — on the device, not on the server. It can reveal where and when you were out. That is why it is passed only to the buddies you are connected with, and only if your contribution is not set to “Only for me”. The complete ride — from your front door back to your front door — stays on your device and is neither transferred nor backed up.

The server matches every contributed line against trails already known, including ones you cannot see. You learn nothing about others in the process: the match only confirms what you uploaded yourself. Only when a buddy has recorded the same trail do you see their contribution.

Feedback becomes public

Feedback from the app is published as an entry in the public GitHub project: your text, the app version and the date, without your username. Who wrote it is stored only in the database and visible to the operator alone. Do not write anything there that should not be public — including trail names, places or route descriptions. Deleting your account does not remove a published entry; contact the operator for that.

Who processes the data

Supabase (processor)

Database, sign-in and the photos with reports (in non-public storage) run at Supabase Inc. on servers in the EU-Central region (AWS, Frankfurt am Main). Supabase processes the data solely on our behalf; the access rules (who sees which row) are enforced in the database itself. For this the app connects to ibxrjgvwouuoydhdkfmf.supabase.co, even before sign-in: at start it reads there which app version is required at minimum. Supabase sees your IP address in the process.

Brevo (processor, sending account emails)

The confirmation email at sign-up, the code for resetting the password and the codes when changing the address are sent via Brevo (Sendinblue GmbH, Berlin). Brevo receives your email address and the content of the email for this.

Signing in with Google, Microsoft, Facebook, GitHub, Discord or LinkedIn (only if you choose it)

Instead of email and password you can sign in with an account at one of these providers. The app opens the provider's sign-in page in the browser; what you enter there is not seen by TrailBuddy. After you agree there, the provider sends your email address, your name (and, depending on the provider, a profile picture) and an identifier of your account with it to our Supabase project. We use only the address and the identifier, to find or create your TrailBuddy account — if an account with that address exists, you sign in to that one. Your username with us is not the provider's name; you choose it in the profile. The provider learns that you sign in to TrailBuddy, but nothing about your trails, rides or buddies. Some providers are based outside the European Union (Google, Microsoft, Meta for Facebook, GitHub, Discord, LinkedIn); their own privacy policies apply to the sign-in page. If you do not want this, sign in with email and password.

Google (processor, notifications)

Notifications are off by default. If you turn them on in the profile, Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging is used — the service through which Android and the browser can deliver notifications at all. Your device creates a random device identifier (“token”), which is stored at Google and with us; it contains neither your name nor your email address. If you turn notifications off again, it is deleted on our side and nothing more arrives.

What a notification contains — and what it does not. A notification passes through Google's servers. It contains the name of the trail (as you see it in the app), the name of the buddy (your alias for them, otherwise their username), what they reported (“closed”, “open again” …) and, if they wrote something with it, the text, cut to 140 characters — plus an internal trail ID the app uses to look the trail up after you tap. If a buddy sends you a message, it contains their name (as above) and the text of the message, cut to 180 characters, plus their internal ID, which the app uses to open the conversation. It contains never any coordinates, no place and no email address. Google may process your device's IP address, and delivery may run through servers outside the European Union. You are only notified of what you are allowed to see in the app anyway: confirmed reports and texts from your direct buddies on trails you can see, and messages to you. If you do not want this to go through Google, leave notifications off — the app shows the same when you open it.

In the browser, a component from Google is added. If you turn notifications on in the browser version, the page loads the program part needed for this from www.gstatic.com, a Google server; your browser necessarily transmits its IP address there. This happens only when you turn them on and never before. The Android app ships the component and does not download it.

Connections the app makes

  • tiles.mcbuchi.de — the map itself. It is stored per region (DACH, southern Canada) as one file (map data from OpenStreetMap, prepared by Protomaps, ODbL) in storage of the operator at Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., located in Western Europe; processor, data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR). The app loads only the pieces the visible section needs. With every map section, your IP address and the requested pieces go to Cloudflare. The app keeps the most recently viewed pieces on the device (at most 100 MB, oldest go first; in the browser in its storage) so that an area can still be seen without reception; they do not leave the device. A piece already stored is not fetched again. Where none is stored, the app shows a bundled overview map without reception, and nothing is fetched for it.
    The same storage holds the places on the map (food and drink, water, bike service, other; data from OpenStreetMap, ODbL), as small files per grid cell of about 11 × 11 km. Only while a group is turned on in the filter — at first that is “Water” — and only from zoom level 12: then the app loads the files of the cells the section touches. Cloudflare receives your IP address and the grid cells, not the exact section — no trails, no rides, no account data. To turn this off completely, deselect all groups in the filter. Up to version 0.17 the places came live from the Overpass API of FOSSGIS e.V.; that connection no longer exists.
    You can save an area for offline use if you want: then the app loads the map pieces, place files and elevation data (Copernicus DEM GLO-90 of the European Union and ESA, prepared by the operator) of that area once from the same storage and keeps them on the device (in the browser in its storage). They do not leave the device and are only deleted when you do so.
    With reception, the app also loads map pieces and elevation data from the same storage that belong to no saved area: when you plan a loop or a route to a trail (can be turned off under “Fill in missing paths online”), when you open a trail without recorded elevations or export it as GPX, and when you import a file with elevations (to compare it with the terrain). Cloudflare receives your IP address and the requested pieces of about 3 × 3 km — no trails, no rides, no account data. The app keeps them only until it is closed.
  • raw.githubusercontent.com — the official trails on the map (public data from authorities, such as the State of Tyrol). Only while the “Official trails” layer is on — at first it is — and only from zoom level 8, when the section touches a region for which there is data: then the app loads that region's file from GitHub and remembers it on the device. GitHub sees your IP address and which region is loaded. No trails from your network, no rides, no account data. This can be turned off with the layer's switch.
  • api.github.com and github.com — the update check queries the project's release list and, on request, downloads the app file. GitHub sees your IP address. This does not apply to the Play Store version.
  • macbuchi.github.io — the web app itself, this page and the legal pages are served by GitHub Pages.
  • fonts.gstatic.com — the web app loads the default font Roboto from Google (a Flutter default), on every page load, before any sign-in. Google sees your IP address.

What else is stored on the device

Session token, settings, a cache of the trails in your network, the loaded official trails and your rides. None of this is backed up to Google's cloud via Android backup; the app explicitly excludes these files. In the web app, the cache, outbox, viewed map pieces and saved areas are stored in your browser's storage (IndexedDB) and do not leave it on their own either. While sign-in waits for the code from the e-mail (forgotten password, confirming the address), the app keeps that e-mail address on the device for at most one hour, so the code entry survives a reload of the page; a password is never stored.

Outbox

If you contribute a recording without a connection, change your contribution to a trail, report something or send feedback, the job waits in an outbox on your device or in your browser's storage and is sent as soon as there is a connection again. It holds exactly what would otherwise have gone to Supabase straight away: the simplified line with elevations, time and source; name, difficulty, character, rating, visibility, description and link of your contribution; or report, condition, time and whether you were on site (yes/no, no position); or text, type and app version of your feedback. The outbox is excluded from Android backup. In the browser, the app asks once, at the first waiting job, that this storage not be cleared on its own; if the browser refuses, the map says so. You can remove any waiting job — trail jobs in the trail list, feedback in the feedback dialog.

Your location

If you tap “My location” or the record button on the map, the app (or the browser) asks once for permission to use your location. With permission, the map shows your position as a dot while the app is open. The location stays on the device: it is transferred neither to Supabase nor to anyone else. Without permission everything else works as before; you can revoke it in the settings of Android or the browser.

Recording a ride (Android only): when you start a ride, the app measures your position every five seconds — also in the background and with the phone in your pocket, through a foreground service with a persistent notification you can always see. Position, accuracy, time and the elevation reported by GPS are measured. The ride is saved as a file on your device (under “My rides” in the profile; that is also where you delete it) and never leaves it on its own — neither to Supabase nor via Android backup. Only if you export it there as a GPX file does it go, through your system's share menu, to the app or recipient you choose. Recording ends with “Stop the ride”, or on its own after twelve hours at the latest. The app does not request background location permission; without a running recording, nothing is measured in the background. During the ride the app compares your position on the device with the trails in your network; if you ride a trail with a report that is not yet confirmed, it asks you by notification whether it is correct. Only your answer goes to Supabase — as a report marked “on site”, as when reporting at the trail — never the position.

Detecting wheelies, jumps and stoppies (Android only, off by default; switch under “About TrailBuddy”): if you turn it on, the app additionally measures the phone's motion sensors (acceleration and rotation, 100 times per second) and the position every second during your next rides, detects wheelies, jumps and stoppies from them on the device and shows them to you. The measurements are kept as a file next to the ride (about 30 MB per hour), are deleted with it and never leave the device on their own — neither to Supabase nor via Android backup. Only if you pass them on under “My rides” with “Share sensor data” do they go, through your system's share menu, to the recipient you choose. The app needs no additional permission for this.

Storage period

  • Account, buddies, recordings and contributions: until you delete them (per trail in the trail sheet under “Delete”: your recordings, your assessment and your reports with their texts on that trail) or delete the account.
  • Reports on trails: 90 days. Your latest confirmed and your latest unconfirmed report and condition on a trail stay longer — the report shown is the latest confirmed one, however old it is — until you delete your contribution or the account.
  • Texts with reports: 90 days. Your latest text on a trail stays longer, until you or a buddy who sees it removes it or you delete the account. The status of a report stays by the rules above, even when its text is removed. Which texts you have already read is remembered only by your device.
  • Photos with reports: 90 days, also with the text that stays longer. It is deleted earlier together with its text — when you or a buddy removes it, or you delete your contribution or your account; the storage is cleaned up every two hours.
  • Messages to buddies: 30 days from sending, then deleted automatically. They are deleted immediately for both sides when a request is declined or withdrawn or a buddy is removed; you can take back your own messages at any time.
  • Error reports: 90 days.
  • Device identifier for notifications: until you turn them off, delete the account or Google declares the identifier invalid (for example after uninstalling); then it is removed on our side.
  • Feedback: indefinitely, see above.

Delete your account

In the app under Profile → Delete account, or without the app following the instructions under Delete your account. The deletion takes effect immediately and removes profile, recordings, contributions, reports with their texts and photos, messages and connections.

Your rights

Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability and objection under Art. 15 to 21 GDPR — by email to the address above. Complaints are handled by the competent data protection supervisory authority; for the controller this is the State Commissioner for Data Protection and Freedom of Information of Baden-Württemberg (Landesbeauftragter für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit).
